# Struct field offsets past an addressing mode's reach

## Documentation

A field read or write lowers to a `[base + disp]` load or store, and the shared lowering
(`StdLoweringShared.memoryDisplacement`) hands the backends the **whole non-negative
`Imm32` domain** — every offset up to `i32.max` — because that is what x64's
`mov reg, [base + disp32]` carries verbatim.

**No ISA is obliged to have a displacement field that wide, and AArch64 does not.** Its
scaled unsigned-offset form carries a 12-bit count of ACCESS UNITS, so what it reaches
depends on the WIDTH of the access:

| access | instruction | scale | largest offset with a direct form |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bool` (and any other byte-wide field) | `LDRB`/`STRB` | 1 | **4095** |
| `Integer`, `float`, a reference — every 8-byte field | `LDR`/`STR` (X or D) | 8 | **32760** |

A field occupies `Project.fieldSlotCount` 8-byte slots — **one** for every declared type
but an **interface**-typed one, which takes two (the 16-byte fat pointer) — so those two
ceilings fall at the **512th** and the **4096th** SLOT: the 512th/4096th ordinary field,
or the 256th/2048th existential one. A struct that reaches them is not exotic — a
generated schema binding or a flag table does — and if the instruction selector did not ask,
the 4097th field's store would PANIC in the encoder:

```
panic: arm64 emitter: load/store offset 32768 (scaled 4096) exceeds the 12-bit unsigned field
```

on a program x64 and wasm32 both compile and run. It is the same shape of defect
`wide-immediate-arithmetic` pins for `+` and `-`: a tier above the backend hands it a
number sized for another target's field, and the backend has to decide per operand
whether a form exists at all.

### The cure is a correct address, never a masked one

When no direct form exists the selector materialises `base + offset` into the IP scratch
register and addresses `[scratch + 0]`, through the same `add`/`sub`-immediate lowering a
wide `x + K` already goes through — so the reach of the folded form is the reach of that
one, stated once. **Masking the offset into the 12-bit field would read or write the
wrong field with nothing to notice it**, which is precisely what the encoder's panic
exists to prevent; the panic stays, as the assertion that the selector did its job.

### Why each case checks a field it did NOT write

An offset that folds to the wrong address is far more likely to hit a NEIGHBOURING field
than to fault, so every case here reads back a field it left at its default as well as
the ones it set. A store that landed one slot over is then a wrong answer with its own
exit code rather than a passing test.
